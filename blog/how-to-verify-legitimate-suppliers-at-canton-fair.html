How to Verify Legitimate Suppliers at the Canton Fair

Updated 12 July 2026 · 6 min read · By Nexera Sourcing and Exim

Quick answer: To verify a Canton Fair supplier, ask whether they are the manufacturer or a trading company, request their business licence and certifications, review factory photos, order samples, and consider a factory visit or third party verification before a large order.

What this guide covers

Manufacturer or middleman

Ask directly whether you are speaking to the manufacturer or a trading company that sources from multiple factories. Manufacturers usually offer better pricing and more direct control over quality, since there is no additional layer between you and production. Trading companies are not automatically a problem, and many are genuinely useful for smaller orders or when they help coordinate multiple factories for a mixed shipment, but you should always know clearly which you are dealing with, since it changes how you should think about pricing, lead time and quality control.

Checks that protect you

  • Request the business licence and relevant certifications for your product category and target market.
  • Ask for factory photos or a short video call showing the production floor, along with stated production capacity and export history.
  • Order samples before committing to any order beyond a small trial size.
  • Use secure payment methods and put agreed terms into a written contract or purchase order before transferring funds.

Go further for large orders

For larger or long term orders, a factory visit or a professional third party inspection service is worth the additional cost relative to the risk of a large shipment going wrong. Our delegation offers supplier verification and factory visits so you deal only with trustworthy partners, and this becomes especially valuable once your order size grows beyond what a sample and a single trial order can fully de-risk.

Questions that separate real factories from resellers

A genuine manufacturer will typically answer detailed production questions specifically and quickly, such as their monthly production capacity for your product, the raw materials they use, and how long a production run of your target quantity would take. A reseller or trading company posing as a manufacturer often gives vaguer answers, redirects to generic marketing language, or hesitates when asked to show the actual factory floor via video call. Asking two or three specific production questions early in a conversation is one of the fastest ways to separate a real factory from an intermediary presenting itself as one.

Documents worth requesting

Beyond the basic business licence, ask for product specific certifications relevant to your target market, such as BIS registration for certain product categories entering India, or CE marking if your onward market requires it. Export history, even informally described, gives useful context on whether the factory regularly ships internationally versus mainly serving the domestic Chinese market, which can affect their familiarity with export documentation and international quality standards.

Verification mistakes to avoid

A common mistake is assuming that simply having a booth at the Canton Fair is proof of legitimacy. Exhibitors go through an organiser vetting process, which is a reasonable starting signal, but it does not replace your own verification of manufacturer status, documents and samples. Another mistake is accepting a verbal claim of certification without asking to see the actual certificate or matching document. A third mistake is rushing verification because a supplier offers an attractive discount for a quick decision, when in fact urgency paired with reluctance to share documents is itself a signal worth taking seriously. Verification scales with order size

Not every order justifies the same depth of verification. A small trial order can reasonably proceed on the basis of a good conversation, sample inspection and basic document checks. A large or recurring order justifies additional steps such as a factory visit or third party inspection. Matching your verification effort to your actual financial exposure keeps the process practical rather than treating every supplier interaction as requiring maximum scrutiny regardless of order size.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know a Canton Fair supplier is genuine?

Confirm they are the manufacturer, review their licence, certifications and factory photos, and order samples before a large order.

Should I visit the factory?

For large or long term orders, yes. A visit or third party inspection reduces risk significantly.

What certifications should I ask a supplier for?

Ask for their business licence, product specific certifications relevant to your market such as BIS or CE where applicable, and export history if available.

Is a booth at the Canton Fair itself proof of legitimacy?

It is a reasonable starting signal since exhibitors are vetted by the organisers, but it does not replace your own verification of manufacturer status and documents.
