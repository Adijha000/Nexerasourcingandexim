How to Schedule Meetings with Vendors Before the Canton Fair

Updated 12 July 2026 · 6 min read · By Nexera Sourcing and Exim

Quick answer: To schedule vendor meetings before the Canton Fair, shortlist exhibitors from the official directory, contact them early with clear product requirements, confirm booth numbers and times, and keep a daily plan. A delegation can arrange verified supplier meetings for you in advance.

Why pre scheduling matters

With tens of thousands of exhibitors spread across the Pazhou complex in any given phase, walking in without a plan wastes entire days on discovery that could have been done from home. Booked meetings turn a five day phase into a focused, productive trip where each day has a clear purpose, and they also signal to suppliers that you are a serious buyer worth prioritising over walk in visitors.

How to do it step by step

  1. Browse the official Canton Fair exhibitor directory for your product category and note company names, booth numbers and halls.
  2. Shortlist target booths, prioritising exhibitors whose product range and certifications most closely match your sourcing needs.
  3. Contact suppliers by email or WeChat with a clear message covering your product needs, target quantity, and preferred meeting day.
  4. Confirm a day and time in writing, and keep a simple daily schedule grouped by hall so your route is efficient.

Writing a message vendors actually reply to

Vendors receive large volumes of enquiries in the weeks before a fair phase opens, and generic messages asking for "your best price" with no specifics are often deprioritised or ignored. A message that states your company name and country, the specific product or product category, your target quantity, and a request for a confirmed meeting time with their booth number is far more likely to get a prompt, useful reply. Keep the message short and specific rather than long and vague, and attach a product reference image if you have one.

Building a realistic daily schedule

Four to six focused meetings per day is a realistic target once you account for travel time between halls, queuing at busy booths, and the time needed for a genuine, useful conversation rather than a rushed handshake. Group your confirmed meetings by hall and zone so you are not crossing the complex repeatedly, and build in buffer time between meetings since good conversations often run longer than planned. Leave the last hour or two of each day unscheduled for walk in discoveries or a second visit to a promising supplier from earlier.

Handling non responses and last minute changes

Not every vendor will respond to a pre-fair message, and this is normal given the volume of enquiries they receive. Follow up once after a week if you have not heard back, and have a backup shortlist ready so an unresponsive vendor does not leave a gap in your schedule. Once at the fair, confirm each meeting the evening before or morning of, since booth staff and schedules can shift, and a quick confirmation message avoids arriving to an empty booth.

What to do once a meeting is confirmed

Once a vendor confirms a slot, add it to your daily itinerary immediately with the hall, booth number and confirmed time, and send a short reminder message the day before as a courtesy that also protects you if the vendor's own schedule shifts. Treat confirmed meetings as fixed anchors around which you fill remaining time with walk-in visits to nearby, unscheduled booths in the same hall.

Frequently asked questions

Can I meet suppliers by appointment at the Canton Fair?

Yes. Many exhibitors welcome scheduled meetings, which saves time versus walking the halls unplanned.

How early should I contact vendors?

Reach out 3 to 5 weeks before the fair so booths can confirm times before their calendar fills.

What should I include in my first message to a vendor?

Your company background, the specific product and target quantity, and a request for a confirmed meeting slot with their booth number.

How many meetings can I realistically fit in one day?

Four to six focused meetings per day is realistic once you include travel between halls and time for genuine conversation at each booth.
