How to Follow Up with Suppliers After the Canton Fair

Updated 12 July 2026 · 5 min read · By Nexera Sourcing and Exim

Quick answer: To follow up after the Canton Fair, contact suppliers within a few days, refer to your booth meeting, request updated quotes and samples, and start with a trial order. Keep clear records so reorders stay consistent.

What this guide covers

  • Move quickly
  • Turn talk into orders
  • Keep it organised
  • What to write in your first follow up
  • Comparing quotes from multiple suppliers
  • Handling slow or silent suppliers

Move quickly

Suppliers at the Canton Fair meet hundreds of buyers across a single phase, so your booth conversation is one of many they will try to recall days later. Follow up within a few days while the meeting is still fresh, and remind them clearly of your booth meeting, the product discussed, the quantity you mentioned and any specific customisation points, so they can pull up the right notes on their end rather than treating your email as a cold enquiry.

Turn talk into orders

  • Request an updated written quote and full specifications, since verbal numbers exchanged at a busy booth can shift once formalised.
  • Ask for samples if you have not already received them, and confirm the cost and shipping timeline for each.
  • Agree payment terms and lead time in writing before any money changes hands.
  • Start with a trial order sized to test quality, packaging and shipping reliability rather than committing to full volume immediately.

Keep it organised

Maintain a simple record, even a basic spreadsheet, of each supplier, their booth number, the quote received, sample status and any specific notes from your conversation. This record becomes essential once you are comparing five or six suppliers a week after the fair, and it keeps your future reorders consistent even if your point of contact at the factory changes.

What to write in your first follow up

A strong follow up email opens by referencing the specific meeting, ideally with a photo of the booth or product attached so there is no ambiguity about which conversation you mean. State the product, target quantity and any customisation discussed, then ask directly for an updated written quote, confirmed lead time, and sample availability with a cost and shipping estimate. Keep the tone professional and brief. Suppliers respond faster to a clear, specific request than to a long, generic message that could have been sent to anyone.

Comparing quotes from multiple suppliers

Quotes from different suppliers are rarely apples to apples unless you standardise the comparison yourself. Normalise every quote to the same order quantity, confirm whether the unit price includes packaging and inland freight to the port, and note the payment terms and lead time alongside price, since a lower unit price paired with a much longer lead time or harsher payment terms may not actually be the better deal. Building this comparison into your simple tracking record from the start saves significant time when you are ready to decide.

Handling slow or silent suppliers

Some suppliers respond slowly after the fair simply because they are working through a large backlog of buyer enquiries. A polite second follow up after a week, referencing your first message, is normal practice and not considered pushy. If a supplier goes fully silent after multiple attempts, treat that as useful information rather than a problem to solve, since a factory that is difficult to reach after the fair is likely to be equally difficult to reach during a live production issue. Redirect your attention to the suppliers who are responsive, since responsiveness itself is a meaningful signal of reliability. Setting a decision deadline for yourself

Give yourself a realistic deadline, typically two to three weeks after the fair, to move from comparing quotes to placing a trial order with your top choice. Buyers who let the comparison process drag on indefinitely often lose momentum, and suppliers who do not hear back within a reasonable window naturally deprioritise the enquiry in favour of buyers who move decisively.

Frequently asked questions

How soon should I follow up after the Canton Fair?

Within a few days, while the meeting is fresh for both sides.

Should I place a large first order?

No. Start with a trial order to confirm quality and reliability before scaling up.

What should a follow up email include?

Reference your booth meeting and photo, restate the product, quantity and target price discussed, and ask for an updated written quote and sample timeline.

How do I compare quotes from multiple suppliers fairly?

Standardise the comparison by unit price at the same quantity, lead time, payment terms and sample cost, since suppliers often quote using different assumptions.
