How Many Days Should You Spend at the Canton Fair?

Updated 12 July 2026 · 5 min read · By Nexera Sourcing and Exim

Quick answer: Most buyers need 3 to 5 days at the Canton Fair to cover one phase properly. If your products span two phases, plan 7 to 10 days. Add travel days and time for factory visits near Guangzhou if you want to inspect production.

What this guide covers

  • One phase, 3 to 5 days
  • Two phases or more, 7 to 10 days
  • Add time for factory visits
  • Matching trip length to buyer type
  • What a 3-day versus a 7-day itinerary looks like
  • A planned itinerary saves days

One phase, 3 to 5 days

A single phase runs about five days and covers an enormous area across the Pazhou complex. Three to five days on the ground lets you meet your shortlisted suppliers, compare several options per product, and negotiate without feeling rushed into decisions on the final afternoon before your flight home.

Two phases or more, 7 to 10 days

If your product categories fall across different phases, for example both electronics and home goods, plan a longer trip of seven to ten days rather than trying to compress everything into a single week. Remember there is a short gap of two to three days between phases while halls are reset, so build that gap into your plan, perhaps using it for factory visits, sightseeing, or simply recovering before the next phase begins.

Add time for factory visits

Serious buyers, particularly those planning larger or recurring orders, often add one or two extra days to visit factories near Guangzhou and inspect production in person beyond what a booth conversation can show. This is one of the highest value parts of the trip for buyers who want real confidence before committing to a sizeable order, since it lets you see actual working conditions, equipment and quality control processes rather than relying solely on booth samples.

Matching trip length to buyer type

A first time buyer exploring a new product category benefits from an extra day compared to an experienced importer, simply to adjust to the scale of the complex and refine a shortlist as new options surface. A buyer testing the waters with a small trial order in one or two categories can often manage well in three to four focused days. A trading company or larger business sourcing across several categories, or planning to combine supplier meetings with factory visits, should plan closer to seven to ten days to do the trip justice without feeling rushed at any stage.

What a 3-day versus a 7-day itinerary looks like

A three day itinerary typically means arriving the evening before the phase opens, spending three full days meeting a tightly shortlisted set of suppliers, and departing the evening the phase winds down, staying entirely within one product category. A seven day itinerary usually spans a full phase for the primary category, a short break during the inter-phase gap for factory visits or rest, and either a second phase or additional supplier follow up meetings before departure. Choosing between these depends less on preference and more on how many categories and how much verification your sourcing goals genuinely require.

A planned itinerary saves days

With meetings booked in advance and a shortlist ready before you land, you can achieve in three focused days what an unplanned visitor struggles to accomplish in six, simply because no time is lost searching for the right halls or waiting for booth staff to become available. Frequently asked questions

Is one day enough for the Canton Fair?

No. One day is not enough to cover a phase. Plan at least three days per phase you attend.

How long is each Canton Fair phase?

Each phase runs about five days, and product categories do not repeat across phases.

Should first time buyers stay longer than experienced buyers?

Yes, slightly. First time buyers benefit from an extra day to adjust to the scale of the fair and refine their shortlist as they go.

How many days should a small trial order buyer plan for?

Three to four focused days is usually enough for a buyer testing one or two product categories with a small trial order in mind.
