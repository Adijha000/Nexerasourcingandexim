Avoiding Canton Fair Scams: How to Spot the Red Flags

Updated 12 July 2026 · 6 min read · By Nexera Sourcing and Exim

Quick answer: To avoid Canton Fair scams, verify that you are dealing with a real manufacturer not a trading middleman, never pay large deposits to personal accounts, insist on samples, and confirm everything in writing. The fair itself is safe, but due diligence on suppliers is essential.

Common red flags

  • Prices far below everyone else for the same product, with no clear explanation for the gap.
  • Pressure to pay a large deposit immediately or to a personal account rather than a company account.
  • No physical factory address, or refusal to share company registration details when asked politely.
  • Vague answers about production capacity, certifications, or minimum order quantities that keep shifting.
  • Reluctance to provide samples, or samples that differ noticeably from what was shown on the booth.

How to verify a supplier

  • Ask directly whether they are the manufacturer or a trading company, and note the answer against what is printed on their booth signage and business card.
  • Request the business licence, relevant certifications, and factory photos or a short video call showing the production floor.
  • Order samples before any large order, even if it adds a few days and a modest cost.
  • Use secure payment methods and put agreed terms in a written contract or purchase order before sending any money.
  • Consider a factory visit or third party supplier verification service for orders above a size that would hurt your business if things went wrong.

Protect your payments

Never send large sums to personal accounts, no matter how convincing the reason given. Use company bank accounts that match the name on the supplier's business licence, prefer secure trade payment methods where available, and structure payments in stages tied to production milestones rather than paying the full amount upfront. Keep records of every message, quote, and invoice, since these records become essential if a dispute arises later and you need to demonstrate what was agreed.

Trader versus factory: why it matters

Not every scam risk involves outright fraud. A large share of supplier confusion at the Canton Fair comes from buyers not realising they are speaking to a trading company rather than the actual manufacturer. Trading companies are not inherently dishonest, and many are genuinely useful for smaller orders, mixed product shipments, or categories where you need help navigating multiple factories. The problem arises when a buyer assumes factory-direct pricing and lead times while actually paying a trader's markup and dealing with an extra layer of communication. Ask the question plainly, and cross check the answer against the certificates and company name on display at the booth.

Scams that happen after the fair, not during it

Some of the riskiest moments happen weeks after the fair ends, not on the exhibition floor itself. A common pattern is a supplier who was perfectly professional in person suddenly pushing for a bank transfer to a new account, citing some urgent reason, once you are back home and the relationship feels established. Another pattern involves a slightly different company name appearing on the final invoice compared to the business card collected at the fair. Treat any change in payment details or company name after the fair as a reason to pause and verify again before transferring funds, exactly as carefully as you would with a brand new supplier.

A simple due diligence checklist

Before placing any order above a modest trial size, confirm the company name on the invoice matches the business licence and the booth signage, confirm the bank account name matches the company name exactly, request at least one sample matching the final agreed specification, and get key terms such as price, quantity, quality standard and delivery date in writing. This short checklist takes a few extra minutes but eliminates the majority of preventable sourcing losses that Indian importers report.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Canton Fair safe for buyers?

The fair is well organised and safe. The main risk is choosing the wrong supplier, which you avoid by verifying manufacturers and protecting payments.

How do I know if a supplier is a real factory?

Ask directly, request the business licence and factory photos, and consider a factory visit or third party verification before ordering.

What should I do if a supplier pressures me to decide immediately?

Walk away or slow down. Genuine manufacturers expect buyers to compare quotes and take samples home, and do not pressure same day payment decisions.

Are trading companies always a scam risk?

No. Many trading companies are legitimate and useful for smaller orders, but you should know whether you are dealing with a trader or a factory so you understand pricing and lead times correctly.
